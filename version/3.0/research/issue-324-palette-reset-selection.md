The chapter 2.0 switch entry inherited a blanket selection of palette 0 on drawing reset. The established #16 palette lifecycle and #105 outer reset derive two distinct operations. SOFT at original `0x16220` calls `0x440f0(-2)` at `0x1636f` to default unprotected RGB records and clear their nonzero definition bits. It can then upload the current colors through `0x3e2e7` at `0x1637e`. Both steps preserve the current index. HARD at `0x16665` clears protection, defaults the records, and explicitly selects zero through `0x442c3(0)`. HEADER global bit 2 requests SOFT and global bit 8 requests an explicit slot-zero selection afterward. Global bit 1 takes reset precedence over bit 2. [Palette matrix](issue-16-table-lifecycle.md#final-native-family-reset-and-backup-checkpoint) · [Outer SOFT trace](issue-105-outer-soft-reset.md) · [HEADER order](../ripscrip/1.1-protocol-and-command-hierarchy.md#global-header-flags).

The `5` in `!|2A500` is the one-digit palette number and `00` supplies two-digit zero flags. Selecting undefined palette 5 initializes and defines it without protection. Wire `*` then resets its RGB values and clears its definition, leaving 5 in the current-index byte. The CUR query reads that byte and INUSE tests the slot's cleared definition. The exact evaluation of `$CUR(PAL)$:$INUSE(PAL,5)$` is `5:0`. The documentation incorporates the earlier conclusions and this wire example.
